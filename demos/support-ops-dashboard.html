Performance Overview

Live data · Last updated just now

If you've never worked inside a support operations team, this dashboard might look like a collection of charts and numbers. Here's what you're actually looking at — explained like I'm walking you through it in person.

This is a performance dashboard I built for a customer support team that was going through a major change: they introduced an AI system to automatically sort and route incoming support tickets to the right agent, instead of doing it by hand. The entire purpose of this dashboard is to answer one honest question — did that change actually make things better for customers, or did it just make the team move faster on paper? Those are not the same thing, and this dashboard is how we tell the difference.

Section by Section

The Six Numbers Across the Top

KPI Cards — your team's vital signs

These are the six numbers that between them tell you almost everything you need to know about whether a support team is healthy on any given day. Let me explain each one in plain language:

CSAT — Customer Satisfaction Score

After a ticket is resolved, the customer gets asked to rate the experience out of 5. Ours is 4.8. That's excellent — most teams consider anything above 4.5 to be world-class.

FCR — First Contact Resolution

The percentage of customers who got their problem fully solved the first time they reached out — without needing to call or email again. Ours is 82.4%. Anything below 70% means you're accidentally creating double the work for yourself.

Avg Handle Time

How long each customer interaction takes from start to finish. Currently 4 minutes 12 seconds — down 18% from last period. Lower is better, but only when satisfaction doesn't drop alongside it. That's the key tension this whole dashboard is built to track.

SLA Compliance

SLA stands for Service Level Agreement — the formal promise you make to customers about how quickly you'll respond. 96.2% of tickets are being handled within that window. The slight dip from last period is what triggered the alert banner at the top.

Open Tickets

47 tickets are currently active and waiting to be resolved. The +12 spike from yesterday is worth watching — that's a 34% jump in a single day. Could be a product issue, a promotion that drove traffic, or just a Monday. Context matters.

Escalation Rate

How often a ticket is too complex for the first agent and has to be handed to someone more senior. At 3.1% and trending down, this means the team is solving more problems on their own — a sign of improving capability over time.

AHT vs. CSAT Trend Chart

Top left — the main argument of the whole dashboard

This is the most important chart on the page. The blue shaded area shows Average Handle Time — how long each customer conversation takes. The green dotted line shows CSAT — customer satisfaction. The red vertical line at Week 4 is when the AI triage model went live.

Here's what the story is: after Week 4, handle time drops sharply — the team is processing tickets significantly faster. But look at what CSAT does. It doesn't dip. It actually climbs. That's the whole point. Speed gains that come at the cost of customer experience aren't gains — they're a trade-off that will hurt you later. When both lines move in your favor at the same time, that's a genuine improvement worth investing in more of.

The annotation marker at Week 4 isn't just decorative — it's a discipline. Without it, someone could look at this chart and attribute the improvement to a seasonal pattern, a new hire joining, or just random variation. The marker eliminates that ambiguity. It says: here is the before, here is the after, here is exactly what changed in between.

Ticket Volume by Category

Top right — where the workload is actually coming from

This donut chart breaks down what kind of problems customers are bringing in. Right now: Technical issues at 38%, Billing questions at 27%, Account access at 16%, Shipping at 11%, Returns at 8%.

This matters far more than most people realize. If Technical suddenly jumps from 38% to 55% in a week, that's not a support team problem — that's an engineering problem in disguise. Something broke in the product, customers are hitting it, and the support queue is the first place it shows up. A support analyst who reads this chart correctly becomes the early warning system between the customer base and the product and engineering teams. That's a much more valuable role than just answering tickets.

SLA Compliance Bar Chart

Bottom left — are we keeping our promises to customers?

Each bar shows what percentage of that week's tickets the team resolved within the committed response window. The dashed horizontal line at 95% is the target. Simple to read: bars above the line, team is on track. Bars below, something needs attention.

Notice that the first two weeks are amber (below target) and every week after the AI launch turns green. The color coding is intentional — a team lead should be able to understand the story in this chart in about three seconds without reading a single number. Design that communicates at a glance isn't cosmetic, it's operational.

Agent Performance Leaderboard

Bottom right — who's performing well, and how do we actually know?

This table ranks agents by a composite performance score — and I want to explain why I didn't just sort by "tickets closed," because that's the mistake most teams make.

An agent who closes 130 tickets a week at 70% First Contact Resolution is generating roughly 39 return contacts — customers who have to come back about the same problem because it wasn't fixed right the first time. Each return contact costs the team time and costs the customer patience. That agent isn't performing well, they're creating hidden debt. Raw ticket count rewards speed. The composite score here rewards actually solving the problem.

The score balances four factors together: how many tickets they handled, their first contact resolution rate, their average handle time, and their customer satisfaction score. The result is a leaderboard that tells you who your genuinely best agents are — not just your fastest ones.

Live Ticket Queue

The table — what the team is working on right now

This is the operational view — every open ticket, who it belongs to, how long it's been open, and most importantly, whether it's at risk of breaching the SLA deadline. Tickets with a red left border and "At Risk" label are the ones that need someone's attention now, not at the end of the shift.

The design principle here is that a team lead should be able to glance at this table for 10 seconds during a busy shift and immediately know where to intervene. Priority and SLA status are the first two columns you see because those are the two things that can blow up your CSAT score by end of day if left unattended. Everything else — category, assignee, status — is context that helps you act on that signal.

# analysis_pipeline.py — ETL: raw tickets → weekly KPIs

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from sqlalchemy import create_engine

sql_query = """
    SELECT
        date_trunc('week', created_at)                    AS week,
        ticket_id, category, agent_id,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (resolved_at - created_at))/60 AS aht_min,
        csat_score,
        first_contact_resolved                            AS fcr,
        escalated
    FROM support_tickets
    WHERE created_at >= NOW() - INTERVAL '90 days'
      AND department = 'Operations'
    ORDER BY created_at ASC;
"""

df = pd.read_sql(sql_query, create_engine(DB_URL))

INTERVENTION_WEEK = '2024-W04'

def build_weekly_kpis(df):
    weekly = df.groupby('week').agg(
        aht_avg     = ('aht_min',    'mean'),
        csat_avg    = ('csat_score', 'mean'),
        fcr_rate    = ('fcr',        'mean'),
        volume      = ('ticket_id',  'count'),
        escalations = ('escalated',  'sum'),
    ).round(2)
    weekly['period'] = np.where(
        weekly.index >= INTERVENTION_WEEK, 'post_ai', 'pre_ai'
    )
    return weekly

def calculate_impact(weekly):
    pre  = weekly[weekly['period'] == 'pre_ai']
    post = weekly[weekly['period'] == 'post_ai']
    return {
        'aht_delta_pct':  round((post.aht_avg.mean()  - pre.aht_avg.mean())  / pre.aht_avg.mean()  * 100, 1),
        'csat_delta':     round( post.csat_avg.mean() - pre.csat_avg.mean(), 2),
        'fcr_delta_pct':  round((post.fcr_rate.mean() - pre.fcr_rate.mean()) * 100, 1),
    }

weekly_kpis = build_weekly_kpis(df)
impact      = calculate_impact(weekly_kpis)
result_json = weekly_kpis.to_json(orient='records', date_format='iso')
# triage_model.py — NLP classifier for auto-routing

from sklearn.pipeline              import Pipeline
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.linear_model          import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.model_selection       import cross_val_score
import joblib

CATEGORIES = ['billing', 'technical', 'account', 'shipping', 'returns']

def build_triage_model(X_train, y_train):
    pipeline = Pipeline([
        ('tfidf', TfidfVectorizer(
            ngram_range=(1, 2),
            max_features=10_000,
            sublinear_tf=True
        )),
        ('clf', LogisticRegression(
            C=5.0, max_iter=1000, class_weight='balanced'
        ))
    ])
    scores = cross_val_score(pipeline, X_train, y_train, cv=5)
    print(f"CV accuracy: {scores.mean():.3f} ± {scores.std():.3f}")
    pipeline.fit(X_train, y_train)
    joblib.dump(pipeline, 'models/triage_v2.pkl')
    return pipeline

def predict_category(model, ticket_text: str) -> dict:
    proba   = model.predict_proba([ticket_text])[0]
    top_idx = proba.argmax()
    return {
        'category':   CATEGORIES[top_idx],
        'confidence': round(proba[top_idx], 3),
        'all_scores': dict(zip(CATEGORIES, proba.round(3)))
    }
# api/endpoints.py — FastAPI: KPI data + triage predictions

from fastapi  import FastAPI, HTTPException
from pydantic import BaseModel
import joblib

app          = FastAPI(title="SupportOps API")
triage_model = joblib.load("models/triage_v2.pkl")

class TriageRequest(BaseModel):
    ticket_id: str
    subject:   str
    body:      str

@app.get("/api/kpis")
async def get_kpis(period: str = "30d"):
    data = fetch_weekly_kpis(period)
    return {
        "kpis":         data.to_dict(orient="records"),
        "impact":       calculate_impact(data),
        "generated_at": datetime.utcnow().isoformat()
    }

@app.post("/api/triage")
async def classify_ticket(req: TriageRequest):
    text   = f"{req.subject} {req.body}"
    result = predict_category(triage_model, text)
    if result['confidence'] < 0.65:
        raise HTTPException(
            status_code=422,
            detail="Low confidence — routing to human review"
        )
    return {"ticket_id": req.ticket_id, **result}
